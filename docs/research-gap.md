# Research gap and evidence needs

## Scope of this statement

This is a repository-level evidence gap, not a claim that a new gap has been established in the wider RF education or wireless research literature. The project has not yet completed a systematic literature review, so it makes no novelty or state-of-the-art claim.

## Current gap

Aether currently makes channel-selection rules visible with a deterministic browser model. It does not connect its displayed choices to measured spectrum activity, packet delivery, radio coexistence, or learner outcomes. The repository therefore cannot answer whether the model predicts physical behavior, how a real device performs, or whether using the simulator improves understanding.

The immediate engineering gap is validation evidence: no browser-level UI checks, fabricated carrier, verified mating-part fit, validated firmware, device-to-device measurements, raw RF data, or user study are recorded. Existing experiment folders contain proposals only.

## Research question candidates

These are unvalidated directions, not approved experiments or claims:

1. Does the deterministic visual model help learners explain channel-selection behavior compared with a text-only explanation?
2. How does an explicitly defined hopping rule affect repeatability and channel-visit distribution in the software model?
3. Under a reviewed, lawful bench protocol, how do a modeled channel sequence and measured device observations differ?

The first question needs an evaluated learning protocol and appropriate participant review. The third requires hardware, lawful operation, reviewed firmware, suitable instruments, calibration, and an independently reviewed safety plan. No question should be treated as a result or as evidence of novelty until related work and methods are reviewed.

## Evidence needed before research claims

- A dated related-work review with inclusion criteria, search sources, and a citation record.
- A precise question, hypotheses or descriptive objectives, and a declared analysis plan.
- Versioned software, hardware, and firmware configurations.
- For physical work: protocol, instruments, calibration, controls, repetitions, raw data, analysis code, and limitations.
- For learning claims: an appropriate study design, participant safeguards, consent/review where required, and predeclared outcome measures.

Until that evidence exists, describe Aether as a learning-oriented simulator and an unbuilt hardware proposal, not as a validated RF testbed or a demonstrated educational intervention.
